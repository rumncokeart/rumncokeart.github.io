September 2026

Six weeks at home, a stack of books, and a long, hard look at what I want.

  • Art projects
  • Reading
  • Watching
  • Travel

September was a different kind of month for me. I was recovering from fibroid surgery, and recovery meant six weeks at home. No exercise, no lifting, no bending. My body had to rest, so the only thing left to do was feed my mind.

It turned out to be a gift. I took a break from social media. I thought a lot about what I want for myself and what I want to shift in my life, and I took a really long, hard look at things. I started reading voraciously, about all kinds of things. And at the same time, I finally finished some of the art projects I had been carrying around.

So this is the first of what I hope becomes a monthly note: what I painted, what I read, what I watched, and where I went.

i.

Art projects

I finished three projects this month.

A textured embroidery

The first was an embroidery project from Wild Floss Embroidery. I loved her textured style. I didn't know you could embroider with all kinds of threads, wool, and yarn. I thought embroidery thread was the only option. So it was good to try this out. It was a beautiful abstract pattern, and I felt a real sense of satisfaction when I finished it.

Shiva in Lippan art

The second was a Lippan art painting of Shiva (Lippan is the mud and mirror work from Kutch in Gujarat). I started it in November 2024 and worked on it on and off since then. This month I finally completed it by adding the mirrors and the paint.

I really love how it turned out, and I'm very satisfied with it. It looks divine, like there's a mountain behind Shiva and the sun is shining on him. It's a gift for my mother-in-law, and I hope she likes it.

Teaching my dad pen and ink

The third was teaching my dad to draw with ink. It took a lot of patience and practice. You dip a stick or a pen in waterproof ink and draw with it, like in the old days. It reminded me of the quills they use in Harry Potter. I really liked teaching him.

ii.

Reading

From Sapta Sindhu to Sapta Konkan: The Incredible Journey of the Gauda Saraswat Brahmans

V. Shenvi Kaisare

A book about Konkanis. I picked it up so I could trace where I come from and my lineage. It was informative and I did learn a few things, but most of it pulls together history from other books and then draws some conclusions. I wish it had gone into much more detail.

Wintering: The Power of Rest and Retreat in Difficult Times

Katherine May

My therapist recommended this one, and it was a great read for me. Usually in winter I seclude myself. I don't want to go out, I don't want to do anything. I always thought of winter and the cold as just hard and annoying.

Now I see it differently. Winter is a time to recharge, to rest, to collect your ideas, to save your breath and go inward. She writes a lot about how nature goes to sleep in winter and how we can take inspiration from that. This book made me fall a little in love with the cold, because now I have new ideas for what to do with it.

I'm really looking forward to winter, and to more memories like the ones I made with Anupam in the snow last year. I really hope it snows this year, even though I hate the cold. I really recommend this book.

A God in Every Stone

Kamila Shamsie

I wouldn't recommend this one. It was very challenging, with lots of characters and lots of history across different generations, but it lacked a coherent thread. The ending felt haphazard, and it wasn't what I thought it would be.

The Covenant of Water

Abraham Verghese

This one is a hundred percent worth reading. I learned a lot about medicine, a lot about India's history, and I loved the story itself.

A small spoiler: one of the main characters loses the use of their hands, and their hands are almost their whole identity. I really empathized with this, because I think about it so often. As an artist, my hands are the most important thing I have for making art, and if, God forbid, something were to happen to them, I would lose my mind. I connected with this book in so many ways.

My husband is a doctor, so I loved telling him about all the medical cases Verghese writes about, and about the story. The ending is a dramatic turn of events, and I really loved reading it.

One Day, Everyone Will Have Always Been Against This

Omar El Akkad

I'm not going to say much about this one. It left me deeply sad about the state of the world, about war anywhere, and about what we are doing to the earth.

Interpreter of Maladies

Jhumpa Lahiri

Definitely worth reading. Short stories that really make you think, and I can see why it won the Pulitzer Prize. I read it in a day and a half. The words are beautiful, and each story is distinct from the others.

iii.

Watching

I'm really into old movies right now, which is great, because I don't really like the newer ones.

  • Shatranj Ke Khiladi (The Chess Players) 1977, directed by Satyajit Ray

    My Kathak teacher, Rachna Nivas, recommended this one. It's a satirical commentary on the nobles of the time, who shied away from their responsibilities while the British took over. It made me think about how much of India's art and culture was lost under British control.

  • Luck by Chance 2009, directed by Zoya Akhtar

    A very well directed movie about how big a role luck plays, but also your choices. It's a big reminder to stand by the people who were with you in your dark times. As we grow into fame, or into who we are, we should always remember where we came from and who brought us there.

  • Abhimaan 1973, directed by Hrishikesh Mukherjee

    Inspired by Pandit Ravi Shankar and his first wife, Annapurna Devi, a brilliant surbahar player who stopped performing in public after he grew jealous of her fame. In the movie the couple are singers. It has a happy ending, which real life didn't, but it was still worth a watch.

  • Thaniyavarthanam 1987, Malayalam, starring Mammootty

    About how a town makes a man mad, even though he isn't, because of a curse on his family, and how his own family turns against him too. Another very thought provoking one.
